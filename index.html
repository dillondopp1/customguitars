Every neck rests for at least 60 days before we carve it

We call the process Quench. Maple is roasted in our own kiln, laminated from five quartersawn strips, then left in a humidity-controlled room for at least 60 days before it is carved by hand.

Roasting drives out moisture and resin. Alternating the grain lets each strip brace its neighbour. The rest gives the wood time to settle before we commit to a carve, and the aim is a neck that stays put through a touring season.

It came out of the repair bench. Elias Marr spent years fixing expensive guitars whose necks moved with the seasons, and Quench is what he does about it. It isn’t patented; it’s simply how we make every neck.

Each guitar leaves with a signed build sheet and a record of where its wood came from

We call the paperwork the Provenance File. It holds the instrument number stamped in the neck heel, your full spec with the final setup measurements (action, relief, pickup heights, weight), the supplier and board ID for each piece of wood, the Quench batch your neck came from, and the signature of the luthier who built it.

While the guitar is being built you can follow it in a private build journal, with photos at every stage from board selection to final setup. If you ever sell it, the next owner gets the file and the journal.

Guitars are numbered in the order they’re finished, and Nos. 1 to 25 make up the Founders’ Run

We number every guitar in the order it’s completed, starting at No. 1, in one sequence across every model. Numbers 1 to 25, in any model and any spec, get a brass number plate on the neck heel and free setups for life. After No. 25, the Founders’ Run closes and never reopens.
