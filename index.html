01 · The neck

Every neck is made to our profile spec, then fretted and set up by hand in our shop

We call the standard Quench. Every neck, maple or mahogany, is made to our profile specs or to measurements you give us. In our shop we level, crown and polish the frets, cut and fit the nut by hand, finish the neck and set it up to your measurements.

Most neck trouble shows up at the frets, the nut and the setup: a high fret, a sharp fret end, a slot cut too deep. That’s the work we do by hand on every guitar. It isn’t patented; it’s simply the standard every Obsidian neck is held to.

How Quench works

02 · The bench

One person finishes, frets and sets up each guitar, and signs for it

The person who takes your spec call writes your spec, then does the finish, the fretwork, the assembly and the 37-point setup on your guitar, and signs the build sheet.

The shop came out of the repair bench. Elias Marr spent years fixing expensive guitars with lifting frets, badly cut nuts and good-looking finishes over sloppy fretwork, which is why the shop’s time goes into fretwork, finish and setup.

About Elias and the shop

03 · The number

Guitars are numbered in the order they’re finished, and Nos. 1 to 25 make up the Founders’ Run

We number every guitar in the order it’s completed, starting at No. 1, in one sequence across every model. Numbers 1 to 25, in any model and any spec, get a brass number plate on the neck heel and free setups for life. After No. 25, the Founders’ Run closes and never reopens.

We build 12 guitars a quarter because that’s what one bench can do properly. Slots open to the waitlist first.

Join the waitlist

04 · The paperwork

Each guitar leaves with a signed build sheet that records its spec, its woods and its final setup

We call the paperwork the Provenance File. It holds the instrument number stamped in the neck heel, your full spec, the species and grade of each wood, the final setup measurements (action, relief, pickup heights, weight), and the signature of the person who finished and set up your guitar.

While the guitar is being built you can follow it in a private build journal, with photos at every stage. If you ever sell it, the next owner gets the file and the journal.
