About Obsidian

One bench. One builder per guitar.

Obsidian is a small workshop that builds electric guitars to order, 12 a quarter. Here’s who builds them, and why the shop works the way it does.

EMPortrait coming soon
The founder

Elias Marr, founder & luthier

Elias learned guitars backwards, by taking broken ones apart. For close to a decade he ran the repair bench at a busy music shop, and the same problems kept coming through the door on expensive instruments: necks that moved every time the seasons changed, frets lifting at the ends, beautiful finishes hiding sloppy fretwork underneath.

He started building at night in a two-bay garage, with a secondhand ceramics kiln bought from a pottery studio that was closing down. The kiln was meant for one experiment: what happens if you roast your own maple instead of buying it pre-roasted, then let it rest before you carve it? That experiment became the Quench process, and the garage became Obsidian.

The name came from the kiln. Obsidian is what lava becomes when it cools too fast to organise itself. Elias wanted a shop that did the opposite: slow, deliberate, one guitar at a time.

What he’d tell you himself: “I’ve fixed thousands of guitars. I know what fails. Everything we build is designed so you won’t have to bring it to someone like me.”

We’re hiring, slowly.

A second luthier will join when the queue justifies it, not before. They’ll build complete guitars, start to finish, and sign their own build sheets. Experienced builders can introduce themselves via the contact page.

How we work

Things we won’t do.

Pay for endorsements

Artists who play an Obsidian bought it or earned it through our artist program. We’ll never list a name that hasn’t played one.

Invent scarcity

12 slots a quarter is what one bench can do properly. When there’s more capacity, we’ll say so.

Hide the paperwork

Your build sheet records every measurement and every board’s source. If we got something wrong, it’s on paper and we’ll fix it.
