How an Obsidian is built, from choosing the boards to the final setup

A machine roughs out the shapes so they’re accurate. Everything you can feel is done by hand, by the one person who signs the build sheet.

The eight stages of a build

One luthier does all eight. CNC roughing is the only stage a machine does.

  1. Board selection

    Every board is weighed, tap-tested and checked for runout. We reject far more than we accept. Its supplier and board ID go on your wood-source record.

  2. Quench

    Neck blanks are roasted in our kiln, laminated, then rested for at least 60 days. We run batches ahead of the queue, so your build never waits on it.

  3. CNC roughing

    A CNC machine cuts the body outline, neck pocket and cavities to within a millimetre. That’s the only machine step in the build.

  4. Hand carving

    Top carves, neck profile and heel are shaped with rasps, scrapers and templates of your measurements, then checked with calipers until they’re right.

  5. Fretwork

    Stainless frets are pressed, levelled, crowned and polished by hand. Ends are rolled so the board edge feels worn-in on day one.

  6. Finishing

    Thin coats, sprayed by hand and wet-sanded between them. Nitro gets weeks of curing before it’s buffed.

  7. Wiring

    Point-to-point wiring with cloth-insulated wire, shielded cavities, and every pickup bench-tested before it goes in.

  8. 37-point setup

    Nut slots, relief, action, intonation, pickup heights, electronics, hardware torque. Each point is measured and recorded on your build sheet.

Quench, the process every Obsidian neck goes through

Obsidian forms when lava is quenched: cooled so fast it turns to glass. We borrowed the name for the stage of the build we’re most stubborn about.

  1. Roast. Maple is roasted in our own kiln on a slow, staged schedule that drives out moisture and resin and caramelises the wood right through.
  2. Laminate. Five quartersawn strips are glued with the grain alternating, so each strip braces its neighbour.
  3. Rest. Laminated blanks sit for at least 60 days in a humidity-controlled room before anyone carves them.
  4. Carve. The profile is hand-carved to your measurements and sealed under a thin satin finish.

Why bother? Roasted maple is more stable, quartersawn laminates resist twisting, and a rested blank has already done most of its moving. We expect a Quench neck to need fewer truss-rod adjustments through the seasons. It’s a process we run in our own workshop, not a patent or a certification, and every batch number is on your wood-source record.

Certificate of authenticity and provenance

Every Obsidian ships with a Provenance File, our certificate of authenticity. It isn’t a third-party certification. It’s our signed record of exactly what went into your guitar.

  • Instrument number, one sequence across every model starting at No. 1, stamped in the neck heel and logged in our build register.
  • Signed build sheet with your full spec and final setup measurements: action, relief, pickup heights, weight.
  • Wood-source record: species, supplier, board ID and the Quench batch your neck came from.
  • Luthier’s signature from the one person who built it, start to finish.

Your build journal

During your build you get a private journal page with photos and notes at six stages: board selection, Quench batch, carve, fretwork, finish, setup. When the guitar ships, the journal is archived with your serial number so a future owner can see how it was made.

We choose wood for how it behaves, and record where every board came from

We use alder, swamp ash, mahogany, korina and figured maple from suppliers who can tell us where each board was harvested. Every board’s supplier and ID goes on your wood-source record. We don’t use endangered species, and we won’t pretend a board is rarer than it is.
