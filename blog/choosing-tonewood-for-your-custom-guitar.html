How to choose the right tonewood for your custom guitar

EM
Elias MarrFounder & luthier · September 4, 2026

Body wood is one of the first choices in any custom build, and one of the most argued about. Here’s a practical guide to the woods we use most, with a straight note on how much they matter.

First, some perspective

On a solid-body electric, the pickups, scale length, construction and setup shape the sound more than the body wood does. Wood still matters: it changes weight, balance and resonance, and the feel of the guitar against your body changes how you play. Choose wood for weight and character, and don’t expect it to transform a pickup’s voice.

Alder

Medium weight, balanced and even, with a slightly scooped midrange. Alder is a versatile default behind countless classic single-coil tones: articulate, snappy, and at home in almost any genre. Standard on our Tephra and Rhyolite.

Swamp ash

Lighter and more open-grained than northern ash, swamp ash is known for clear highs and a full low end. It suits players who want sparkle in clean tones and bite when they dig in. Its grain looks striking under a translucent finish.

Mahogany

Denser and warmer, with strong low-mids. Mahogany is the backbone of many humbucker-driven rock tones. Under a carved maple cap, as on our Basalt, you get warmth from the back and definition from the top.

Figured maple (flame & quilt)

Usually used as a top rather than a whole body. Maple is hard and bright, and a carved cap tightens the low end of a warm body wood. Flame and quilt figure are where most of a guitar’s visual drama comes from. In our pricing that’s the Master and Reserve wood grades.

Korina

Light, even-grained and resonant, with a woody, slightly scooped voice. A favourite for players who want vintage character without mahogany’s weight. We offer it on the Rhyolite and Scoria.

So which should you choose?

Start with the music you play and the weight you want on your shoulder. Want articulate cleans and versatility? Alder or swamp ash. Live in high-gain territory and want weight behind palm mutes? Mahogany with a maple cap. Want light and lively? Korina, or a chambered body. Still unsure? Tell us what you play in your build request. We’ll recommend a pairing on your spec call, and the board we choose goes on your wood-source record.
