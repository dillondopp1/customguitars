How To Choose The Right Tonewood For Your Custom Guitar

One of the first decisions you'll make in the Custom Shop is which wood your guitar is built from — and it matters more than most players expect. Here's a practical breakdown of the woods we work with most, and how each one shapes your tone.

Alder

Balanced and even across the frequency spectrum, with a slightly scooped midrange. Alder is the workhorse choice behind countless classic single-coil tones — articulate, snappy, and versatile across genres. A great default if you're not chasing a specific extreme.

Swamp Ash

Lighter and more porous than standard ash, swamp ash offers bright top-end clarity with a surprisingly full low end. It tends to have an airy, resonant quality that cleans up beautifully and still has bite when you dig in.

Mahogany

Warm, dense, and full of midrange weight. Mahogany bodies push more low-mid presence and natural compression, which is why it's the backbone of so many humbucker-driven, high-gain tones. Paired with a maple cap, you get warmth from the body and clarity from the top.

Figured Maple (Flame & Quilt)

Typically used as a top rather than a full body, figured maple adds brightness, hardness, and visual drama. A flame or quilted maple cap over mahogany is a classic combination for a reason — it tightens up the low end while keeping the warmth underneath.

Korina

Light in weight but surprisingly resonant, korina delivers a woody, slightly scooped tone with excellent sustain. It's a favorite for players who want vintage character without the weight of mahogany.

So Which Should You Choose?

Start with the music you play, not the wood you've read about online. If you're chasing articulate cleans and funk/rock versatility, alder or swamp ash is a safe bet. If you live in high-gain territory and want weight behind your palm mutes, mahogany with a maple cap is hard to beat. Not sure? Tell us the artists and tones you're chasing in your Custom Shop request — our luthiers will recommend a wood pairing for your spec at no extra cost.
