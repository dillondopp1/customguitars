Nitro vs. poly: what a guitar finish really changes

EM
Elias MarrFounder & luthier · October 9, 2026

Few spec decisions start more arguments than the finish. Here’s what nitrocellulose lacquer and modern polyurethane actually do differently, including the parts that matter less than people think.

The two finishes in one paragraph each

Nitrocellulose lacquer (“nitro”) was the standard finish on electric guitars through their early decades. It dries as its solvents evaporate, and each new coat partly melts into the one beneath, so it builds into a single thin film. It stays relatively soft, and it ages: it yellows, wears through where your arm rests, and can develop fine “checking” cracks with sudden temperature changes.

Polyurethane (“poly”) covers a family of modern finishes that cure chemically into a hard, tough, flexible film. It resists sweat, scratches, chemicals and temperature swings, and it looks the same in twenty years as it does on day one. Production guitars often use thick polyester or poly coats because they’re fast and durable. A carefully applied poly finish can be thin too.

Side by side

DurabilityPoly is far tougher; nitro dents, wears and checks
AgeingNitro wears in and changes colour; poly stays as built
FeelThin nitro feels “woody”; satin poly feels similar; thick gloss poly can feel plasticky
Cure timeNitro needs weeks before buffing; poly is ready in days
RepairsNitro repairs blend in invisibly; poly repairs are harder to hide
Stands & strapsNitro can react with some rubber and vinyl; poly doesn’t
CostNitro takes more labour and booth time

What about tone?

The argument for nitro is that a thinner, softer film damps the wood less. There may be something in it for acoustic instruments, where the top’s vibration is the sound. On a solid-body electric, the pickups hear the strings, and any finish effect is subtle at best. What we can say with confidence is that thickness matters more than chemistry. A thin poly finish and a thin nitro finish will be closer to each other than either is to a thick, dipped production finish.

Who should choose nitro

  • Players who want a guitar that visibly records their playing over the years.
  • Anyone chasing a vintage look, including checking and wear.
  • Players happy to use nitro-safe stands and avoid leaving the guitar in a hot car.

Who should choose poly

  • Touring players and anyone who sweats heavily or plays outdoors.
  • Anyone who wants their translucent burst to look exactly the same in 2046.
  • Players with a shorter timeline, since poly cures faster.

How we price it

Satin or solid-color gloss poly is included in every Obsidian base price. Caring for each

Nitro: use stands and straps labelled nitro-safe, avoid rapid temperature changes (let the case warm up before opening it in winter), and wipe it with a dry microfibre cloth. Poly: almost anything goes. A damp cloth and an occasional polish is all it needs.
