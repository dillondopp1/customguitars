# Research report: premium guitar demand and the piano-key resin line

Prepared October 2026 for Obsidian Guitars. **Not legal advice.** Get the legal sections reviewed by a wildlife-trade attorney and/or the USFWS Office of Law Enforcement before buying any stock.

**Evidence key:** [S] sourced fact · [E] our estimate or inference · [A] anecdotal (forum post, single listing, opinion)

---

## Recommendation

**Go, as a limited, numbered run, but with no ivory, a resin top over a wood body, and pre-sales first.**

1. **Never sell guitars containing elephant ivory.** A guitar built in 2026 cannot use either federal exception (de minimis or antique). It fails every state musical-instrument exemption we found that is tied to the instrument's build date, and it effectively cannot be sold into the EU or UK. Remove the ivory key tops and keep them out of the product. The legal section below has the details.
2. **Build a keys-in-resin top over a chambered wood body. Do not build solid epoxy bodies.** A solid epoxy body puts the guitar at about 13–14 lb. A 6–10 mm keys-in-resin top over chambered alder or mahogany keeps it around 7.5–8.5 lb. [E]
3. **Launch it as a numbered limited run, not a permanent model.** In this market, guitars sold on their materials and story sell when they are scarce and have provenance. PRS's Reclaimed run sold out; Ormsby's order windows sell out. [S] Standing novelty models have no proven resale.
4. **Prove demand first.** Pre-sell 3–5 numbered builds with deposits before committing workshop capacity. Piano-key guitars have no public resale record, and the one existing catalog model shows no reviews. [S]
5. **Proposed name and position:** a separate "Obsidian Reliquary" run (working name; run the same trademark search as the model names). Each guitar is documented back to one dated donor piano.

## Part 1: What sells at $3,000–$10,000

### The market right now
- **Long-run demand is real.** NAMM reports US fretted-instrument sales up 38% over 10 years. [S]
- **The pandemic boom is over.** Reverb's price index has used electric guitar prices about 10% below their peak in early 2025. Fender has cut volumes and raised prices twice in 2025 because of tariffs, and dealers are keeping inventory lean into 2026. [S] A reviewer called it "not a great time to sell expensive handmade gear to hobbyists." [A]
- **No reliable figures exist for the boutique or custom-shop segment.** Vendor estimates of the overall market range from $0.5B to $4.5B, which makes them unusable. [S]

**What this means for Obsidian [E]:** the $4.8k–$6.4k base prices are in a flat-to-soft market where buyers are sensitive to price. Your buyer also compares against boutique guitars on Reverb selling 20–40% under new. [A]

### Competitors in the price band [S unless marked]
| Builder | Typical price |
|---|---|
| Suhr Modern Custom | ~$3.9k–4.5k |
| Fender Custom Shop relic | ~$4.5k–5.6k |
| Gibson Murphy Lab | $4.3k–10.5k |
| Strandberg Custom Shop | ~$5.9k–6.25k |
| Aristides 060 / NAMM 2026 runs | from ~$3.5k / $5.15k |
| Ormsby Custom Shop | from AUD 3,990, typically ~AUD 5k, 6–12 months, sold in order windows |
| PRS Private Stock | ~$14k+ |

The busiest part of the band is $3.5k–6k. [E] Obsidian's base pricing competes head-on with Fender and Gibson Custom Shop guitars, which have strong brands and established resale values. **The base line needs a reason to exist beyond "hand-built."** The piano-key run can supply that story.

### What makes people buy
- **Vintage feel and aging:** buyers pay heavily for relic finishes. Gibson charges about £2k+ more for Heavy aging than VOS. [S]
- **Scarcity:** order windows and limited runs sell out. [S]
- **Headless and ergonomic designs** are clearly growing, though no sales data exists. [S, qualitative] This supports the Scoria.
- **Value for money:** buyers "save for longer to get a great instrument." [S]
- **Resale value:** dealers say Suhr, Anderson and Collings hold value. [A] A new brand has no resale record, and that is a real objection buyers will raise.
- **Artists and YouTube demos:** plausible drivers, but nobody has measured their effect. [E]

### Unusual materials and story-driven guitars
- **Reclaimed or historic wood works as limited runs:** PRS Reclaimed (sold out; S2 run of 700 in 2025), Fender reclaimed pine and redwood, and Wallace Detroit's 12 firehouse-floorboard guitars. [S]
- **Rick Kelly's Carmine Street guitars** (built from New York landmark lumber) sold new for ~$2k–2.5k; one used example was listed at $3,995. [S] Provenance holds value.
- **Epoxy "river" guitars** are mostly viral one-offs, and there is no resale data for them. [S/A]
- **Pattern [E]:** a material story sells when it has (a) credible provenance or a big brand behind it and (b) a hard limit on numbers. Nothing shows novelty guitars resell better. They probably resell to fewer buyers.

### Who buys and where
- **Buyers [E, dated evidence]:** mostly affluent amateur players aged about 40–65 ("doctors, lawyers"). Some dealers report a growing share of buyers under 50. [A]
- **Where they buy:** specialist dealers (The Music Zoo, Chicago Music Exchange, Wildwood) or direct from builders with order windows (Ormsby, Kiesel). Reverb is where resale value gets set. [S]
- **For a new brand [E]:** sell direct with order windows plus demos, and aim to place guitars with one or two credible boutique dealers once you have a reputation.

## Part 2: The piano-key resin line

### Who already does this
| Maker | What | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Gepetto's Guild "Piano Guitar" | T-style body with ~100-year-old piano keys cast in clear epoxy; a standing catalog model; 9–9.5 lb | $3,250 (one Reverb listing $3,400) [S]; no reviews or artist users found |
| "Nocturne 88 Deluxe" (Etsy) | Clear resin T-style body with keys from a 1920s baby grand | ~AU$3,521 [S, search snippet] |
| Pepe Romero Jr. "Baby Grand Project" | Early-1900s Steck soundboard turned into 9 ukuleles and 2 mini guitars, sold through Guitar Salon International | the best example of a provenance-led run [S] |
| Jake Fuller (UK), Scavenger Music | Guitars built from old piano wood | one-offs [S] |
| Yamaha "Upcycling Guitar – Piano" | Concept guitar from piano-factory offcuts, Red Dot Design Concept award 2024 | not sold [S] |

**What this means [E]:** the idea is not new, and the only existing catalog model is around $3,250. Obsidian's differentiator has to be **documented provenance plus craft at a higher level**: one dated donor piano per run, a full materials record, and a Quench neck. Novelty alone won't carry the price.

### What 100-year-old keys are made of
- **Key sticks:** spruce or basswood. [S]
- **Sharps:** ebony, or a cheaper wood stained black. [S]
- **White tops:** elephant ivory on most good pianos of the period. Cheaper pianos used celluloid ("Ivorine", "Pyralin"). [S]
- **Telling them apart [S, informal methods]:**
  - Ivory tops are usually two pieces with a joint line; celluloid tops are one piece.
  - Ivory has a fine natural grain; ivorine shows printed-looking stripes.
  - Hot-pin test: a hot pin craters plastic but barely marks ivory.
  - For certainty, get a conservator to identify them.
- **Celluloid risks:**
  - It is highly flammable. [S]
  - It breaks down with age and gives off nitric acid. [S]
  - It softens at about 80–90 °C. [S] A thick epoxy pour can approach that heat as it cures. [E]
  - **Leave celluloid out of the castings.** [E]

### Sourcing [S unless marked]
- **Cost:** old uprights are free or close to it. Owners often pay $200–300 to have one removed, and many end up scrapped.
- **Channels:** piano technicians, rebuilders, PianoAdoption, free listings and auctions.
- **Cost per piano [E]:** about $0 plus $150–400 for moving and disposal, and 4–8 hours to take it apart.
- **Yield [E]:**
  - One 88-key set gives 2–4 resin top caps, or 10+ inlay packages for fretboards and headstocks.
  - The soundboard and lid wood give you more provenance material.
  - Supply is plentiful. The bottleneck is labor.

### Construction plan
| Build | Body weight [E] | Full guitar [E] |
|---|---|---|
| Alder body (reference) | ~4.0 lb | ~7.5–8 lb |
| Solid epoxy body | ~10 lb | ~13–14 lb ✗ |
| Keys packed in resin | ~6.5–8 lb | ~10–11 lb ✗ |
| **6–10 mm keys-in-resin top on chambered alder or mahogany** | **~4.2 lb** | **~7.5–8.5 lb ✓** |

**How to build it:**
- **Heat:** a 6–10 mm pour is far inside deep-pour epoxy limits of 2–4 in. per pour, so cure heat is low risk. [S]
- **Bubbles:** seal the keys with a thin coat of epoxy before the main pour, then degas or pressure-pot the cast. [E]
- **Movement:** use acclimatized wood and a tough, slightly flexible casting resin. The wood keeps moving with humidity while the cured resin does not, so expect stress cracks otherwise. [E]
- **Hardware:** cut the neck pocket, pickup cavities and bridge screws into wood, not resin. [E]
- **Finish:** use a UV-inhibited clear topcoat. All epoxy yellows over time. [S]
- **Tone:** a thin resin top has little effect on tone. No controlled studies exist. [E]

**Product tiers [E]:**
- **Signature:** a resin top over a chambered wood body.
- **Accessible:** ebony sharps used as fretboard markers, a headstock overlay or pickguard inlay.

### Provenance package (what buyers actually pay for)
- **Dating:** date each donor piano by serial number using the Pierce Piano Atlas and maker charts. [S]
- **Records:**
  - photos of the piano's cast-iron plate and maker's decal
  - teardown photos
  - the previous owner's story, where available
- **Paperwork:** ship a signed certificate and a materials statement. The statement must say "contains no ivory" and identify the species of the sharps.
- **Run naming:** name the run after the piano itself, e.g. "Reliquary No. 1 — 1912 upright, serial ####."
- **Trademarks:**
  - A factual line such as "keys from a 1911 Steinway upright, serial ####" is probably acceptable as a plain factual reference to the brand. [E]
  - Never put a piano brand in a model name, never use a piano maker's logo or font, and never imply endorsement.
  - Steinway enforces its trademarks aggressively. [S] Have counsel review the marketing copy.

### Pricing [E]
| Tier | Price |
|---|---|
| Inlay-only (sharps and key veneers) | base + $600–1,200 → ~$5.4k–7.6k |
| Signature resin-top run | base + $1,800–3,500 → **~$6.9k–8.9k** |
| Named "historic donor" one-offs (notable piano or owner) | $9k+ |

**Why the premium:** each guitar takes 25–45 extra hours [E], early castings will have a high failure rate, the donor material is finite, and the provenance paperwork takes work. The ~$3.3k comparable means Obsidian must visibly out-build it.

## Part 3: Legal and regulatory (not legal advice)

### Federal elephant ivory rules
- **Selling or offering ivory across state lines or abroad is banned** (50 CFR 17.40(e)(3)) unless an exception applies. [S]
- **De minimis exception (items under 200 g of ivory):** it requires, among other things, that the item was **manufactured before July 6, 2016**. A 2026 guitar fails that outright. [S]
- **Antique exception:** it requires the *article* to be at least 100 years old and not modified with ivory since 1973. A new guitar can't meet that.
  - FWS's own guidance for piano technicians says that putting salvaged ivory into another piano loses antique status. [S]
- **Asian elephant ivory** has no de minimis route at all. Telling the species apart in thin key tops needs lab work. [S]
- **Sales within one state** are outside the federal ban. [S] However, state law often forbids them, and an online listing visible nationwide may count as an interstate offer. That point is unsettled. [interpretation]

### State bans [S]
- **New York:** needs a DEC permit and an instrument made no later than 1975.
- **California:** less than 20% ivory, instrument documented as made no later than 1975. Mammoth ivory is banned too.
- **Washington:** less than 15% ivory by volume. A violation over $250 is a felony.
- **Oregon:** exempts an instrument part under 200 g with no build-date limit. This is unclear for a new instrument; ask counsel.
- **Also reported as banning ivory:** NJ, HI, IL, MD, MN, NV, NH, NM, VT. Their exemptions are unverified.
- **Every build-date-based exemption fails for a 2026 guitar.**

### International [S]
- **CITES Musical Instrument Certificates** cover only non-commercial travel, never sales.
- **EU (2022)** and **UK (Ivory Act 2018)** instrument exemptions apply only to instruments made before 1975. UK penalties run up to £250k or 5 years in prison.
- **Seizure:** undocumented ivory is forfeitable at customs.

### Woods in old pianos
- **Brazilian rosewood** veneer is CITES Appendix I, with no instrument exemption. [S]
- **Madagascar ebony** is under a trade suspension; other ebonies are not listed. [S]
- **The Lacey Act** turns any state or foreign violation into a federal offense. It was the basis of the 2011 Gibson raid. [S]

### Enforcement precedent [S]
- **A440 Pianos (GA)** pleaded guilty in 2010 over ivory-topped piano keys hidden in crates. The company and its CEO were each fined $17,500 and given 3 years' probation.
- **ESA penalties:** criminal up to $50k and/or 1 year in prison; civil penalties per violation are adjusted for inflation each year.

### Compliance plan
1. **Separate the ivory before teardown.** Every key top is assumed to be ivory until a test shows otherwise. Remove all ivory tops and keep a log.
2. **Store or donate the removed ivory. Never sell it across state lines.** Ask the USFWS Office of Law Enforcement about donation.
3. **Use only:** wooden key sticks, ebony or ebonized sharps (confirm they are not Madagascar ebony if exporting), soundboard and case woods, and confirmed non-celluloid tops. Check any rosewood veneer for Brazilian rosewood.
4. **Prefer post-ivory pianos (1950s–70s+)** for white-key material, or use only the wood from older pianos.
5. **Celluloid:**
   - Leave it out of castings.
   - Don't ship scrap: raw and scrap celluloid are hazmat (UN2000/UN2002).
   - Sand it only with fire precautions in place.
6. **State it clearly:** put "contains no ivory or endangered species materials" on the certificate, the product page and the export paperwork.
7. **Get counsel to review** the materials statement, piano trademark references and export plan before launch.
