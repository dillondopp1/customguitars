# Instagram static posts: research brief for Obsidian Guitars

Purpose: give Claude Code (working with ChatGPT image generation and Canva) concepts to adapt for this month's Instagram posts. Written 21 Nov 2026.

**Read this first.** The research below came from web searches of marketing blogs and vendor studies. I could not browse Instagram feeds or the Meta Ad Library directly, and no source gave conversion data for made-to-order guitar builders. Everything under "What the research says" is directional. The concepts in section 3 are my adaptation of those findings to this brand, not proven winners. Treat them as a test plan (section 6).

---

## 1. What the research says

**Format**
- Carousels beat single images on engagement and saves in nearly every 2026 source. Figures vary by study: 0.52% vs 0.35% engagement (Q1 2026 benchmark), and saves from "35% more" up to "9x" depending on vendor. Single-image reach and engagement are reported as falling year over year. An independent audit of 1,686 posts was more modest: carousels drew 1.12x an account's usual post, single images 0.88x. Sweet spot is 4-6 slides for engagement, 7-10 for saves.
  - Implication: make most "static" posts **multi-slide carousels** (3-6 slides), with a few strong single images.
- Export at **1080 x 1350** (4:5 portrait).
- Sources: [mean.ceo](https://blog.mean.ceo/instagram-carousel-engagement-statistics/), [fadetoblack.io](https://www.fadetoblack.io/blog/instagram-carousels-vs-single-images), [instapect.com audit](https://instapect.com/blog/instagram-carousel), [adpicto.com](https://www.adpicto.com/en/blog/instagram-carousel-best-practices-2026)

**Ranking signals**
- Instagram's head named watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as top signals. Sends (a person DMing the post to a friend) are widely reported as the strongest signal for reaching new people. Saves and shares are weighted above likes. Order of importance is disputed, and weights like "5x a like" are unverified.
  - Implication: design posts people **send to a friend** ("this is the one I'd want") or **save** (spec guides, finish charts, "how to choose").
- Sources: [Buffer](https://buffer.com/library/instagram-algorithm/), [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/instagram-algorithm)

**Design for saves**
- Bold headline plus supporting text, 2-3 colours, generous empty space, numbered lists. Hook slide should create an information gap. Specific CTAs ("Save this for when you spec your build") beat generic ones ("let me know what you think").
- Sources: [dev.to carousel guide](https://dev.to/fermainpariz/how-to-create-instagram-carousel-posts-that-get-saved-and-shared-2026-guide-1jjm), [contentstudio.io](https://contentstudio.io/blog/instagram-photo-trends)

**What converts in DTC ads (applies to organic creative too)**
- Most-cited formats: UGC-style testimonials and before/after. Static is described as cheaper per acquisition and best for retargeting; video wins cold prospecting. Testimonial carousels are reported 15-30% above single-image retargeting ads. Label before/after states with text because many views are silent. Several of the "4x CTR UGC" claims cite each other and could not be traced to a primary source.
- Sources: [segwise.ai](https://segwise.ai/blog/dtc-ad-creative-hooks), [askneedle static vs video](https://www.askneedle.com/blog/static-vs-video-ads-what-actually-converts-for-dtc-brands), [adlibrary.com](https://adlibrary.com/posts/best-dtc-meta-ads-examples-2026)

**Made-to-order and high-ticket makers**
- Custom orders, waitlist requests and "is this available" questions mostly arrive **by DM**, so the post must say what to do ("DM us 'BUILD'"). Limited-run "drop" posts and made-to-measure explainers are the recommended maker formats. Use a structured intake (model, finish, timeline).
- Luxury guidance: fewer, more deliberate posts beat volume; a post with fewer impressions but high saves is worth more. Flatlays, close-ups and detail shots dominate watch and jewellery imagery.
- Sources: [socialmon.ai leather maker examples](https://www.socialmon.ai/blog/61-leather-goods-maker-instagram-post-ideas-with-examples-that-sell), [brandingstrategyinsider.com](https://brandingstrategyinsider.com/?p=35346), [Sprout Social via nationaljeweler.com](https://nationaljeweler.com/articles/5002-6-insights-into-standing-out-on-social-media)

**Guitar niche**
- Premier Guitar argues the boutique market has become image-driven and visually samey (stained burl tops, pre-distressed finishes blur together). Differentiation therefore comes from **specificity and restraint**, which is already this brand's positioning. Time-lapse and builder-in-DMs are the recurring builder tactics. I found no brand-level data for Fender, Gibson, PRS, Taylor or boutique builders.
- Sources: [Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/pro-advice/esoterica-electrica/has-the-boutique-market-peaked), [Breakthrough Guitar](https://breakthroughguitar.com/why-boutique-guitar-builders-are-outselling-major-brands-in-2026/)

**Meta ad library signal**
- Longest-running ads are the best public proxy for a profitable ad (e.g. GuitarTricks' top headline ran across 117 ads for up to 269 days). Offer-led, benefit-led copy dominates the music-education side. [admanage.ai](https://admanage.ai/ad-library/guitartricks.com)

## 2. Brand rules that override any inspiration (from docs/BRAND.md)

Claude Code must follow these when writing captions and on-image text:
- Positioning line: *Electric guitars, finished one at a time by the person who signs for them.* Proof comes from process and paperwork, not adjectives.
- **Never name or mention suppliers; don't volunteer "outsourced".** Say what is true: design is ours, components are made to our spec, each guitar is finished, fretted and set up by hand in our shop by one person who signs the build sheet, 37-point setup and inspection.
- **Never claim:** board selection, in-house kiln or roasting, laminated/five-piece necks, neck rests, hand-carved necks or tops, chambering/routing by the shop, or one luthier building "start to finish".
- Quench is a neck *standard*, not a process or technology.
- Founders' Run (Nos. 1-25) is a launch price, **never a discount gimmick: no countdowns, no "only X left", no dates for when it fills.**
- Lead time: 16-24 weeks from deposit plus about 4 weeks for nitro.
- Don't use "Strat", "Tele", "Les Paul". Use S-style, T-style, single-cut.
- Real photography is the biggest lever. Current product images are AI-generated studio photos and illustrated renders. Don't caption stock or AI images as customer builds, the founder, or "our workshop". Label renders honestly. Founder persona: Elias Marr, repair-bench origin.
- Don't invent testimonials, customer names, player endorsements, view counts or reviews. There are no reviews yet, so the testimonial format below must wait for real ones.

## 3. Static post concepts to adapt

Each concept: what it is, why it should work, and how to build it with real material. Slide counts assume 1080 x 1350.

1. **The spec guide carousel (save bait).** "Roasted maple vs mahogany necks: how to choose." 5-7 slides, one decision per slide, last slide a soft CTA. Source basis: saves-oriented educational carousels. Uses facts from the brand doc (mahogany is +$150 on most models; roasted maple is standard).
2. **Finish chart.** Swatch grid of the actual finish options (solid gloss, satin, translucent, hand-shaded burst, nitro) with the upcharge printed. Pricing transparency is a differentiator and a natural save.
3. **"What's on the build sheet."** Close-up of the Provenance File: instrument number, wood species and grades, setup measurements (action, relief, pickup heights, weight), signature. Carousel: one section per slide. Proof-not-adjectives, which is the stated positioning. Use a real or clearly mocked-up sheet.
4. **37-point setup, one point per slide.** Pick 5-6 points (nut slot depth, fret end dressing, relief, action, pickup height, intonation). Detail shots, big numerals.
5. **Fretwork macro.** Single image: a crowned and polished fret, close-up, dark background, one line of text. Watch and jewellery brands rely on close-ups for the same reason: they signal care.
6. **Model lineup comparison.** "Five shapes, five rocks." One slide per model (Andesite, Tephra, Rhyolite, Basalt, Scoria) with the one-line stat from the name story and base price. Last slide: "Which one fits how you play?" CTA to DM.
7. **"Who is this for?" posts.** Per model: player type, typical use, one thing it does better than a production guitar. Sendable to a bandmate.
8. **Founders' Run explainer.** What the first 25 get (low number, brass plate, free setups for life, launch price). Calm and factual. No urgency devices (see rules).
9. **Process-of-ordering carousel.** Deposit, spec call, components ordered, finish, fretwork, setup, signed build sheet, ships. Includes the 16-24 week range. Reduces the "what happens after I pay" worry that blocks four-figure purchases.
10. **Founder note post.** Elias Marr, repair-bench origin: why setups and fretwork are the focus. Single image plus a long caption. Needs a real photo of the founder at the bench. Do not use stock or AI for this.
11. **Myth/fact post.** "A $5,000 guitar still needs a setup." Counter-intuitive hook, one slide of reasoning, one slide of what our setup covers.
12. **Before/after (once real builds exist).** Rough fret ends vs dressed fret ends, or a neck before and after final fretwork, with both states labelled in text. This is the strongest format in the DTC research, and it needs real photos of real work.
13. **Customer spec stories (later).** Only after real customers have agreed. Quote, their spec list, a photo of their guitar. Don't fabricate.
14. **Quiet single-image product shots.** One guitar, dark seamless background, warm key light, minimal text (model name and one fact). Two of these a month is enough. Restraint is the luxury signal.

**Calls to action that fit a made-to-order shop:** "DM us BUILD to talk specs", "Save this for your spec sheet", "Send this to the friend who's been eyeing a custom". Link in bio to the custom-shop form.

## 4. Suggested monthly mix (approx. 12 posts, not more)

| Share | Type | Concepts |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Educational carousels (saves) | 1, 2, 4, 6, 9 |
| 2 | Proof posts (build sheet, fretwork) | 3, 5 |
| 2 | Offer/explainer | 7, 8 |
| 1 | Founder | 10 |
| 2 | Quiet product shots | 14 |

Luxury guidance favours fewer, better posts over volume, so 12 good ones beat 30 average ones.

## 5. Production notes for ChatGPT and Canva

- Canvas 1080 x 1350. Palette: 2-3 colours from the site (dark mat plus one warm accent). One typeface pair across every post so the grid reads as one system.
- Slide 1 is a hook; every slide has one idea; last slide has one CTA.
- Keep on-image text short. Put the detail in the caption.
- AI-generated guitar imagery tends to get hardware, fret counts and headstocks wrong. Check each image against the real model spec before using it, and prefer the existing photos in `assets/`.
- Don't generate faces, hands "building" a guitar, or workshop scenes and imply they're ours.

## 6. How to find out what actually converts

1. Tag each post with its concept number and CTA.
2. After 2-3 weeks compare **saves, sends and DMs/form submissions per post** (not likes).
3. Double down on the concept types with the most sends and DMs; drop the bottom third.
4. For real competitor inspiration, spend 30 minutes in the [Meta Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library) (Image filter, sort by longest-running) searching Suhr, Knaggs, Tom Anderson, Strandberg, Mayones and Sweetwater, and save the best 10 into a swipe file. That is the one piece of research I couldn't do from here.
